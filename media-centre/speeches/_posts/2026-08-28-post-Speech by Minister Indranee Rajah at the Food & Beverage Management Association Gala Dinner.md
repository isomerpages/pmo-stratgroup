<p>Mr Lens Gwee, President, Food &amp; Beverage Management Association</p>
<p>FBMA Executive Committee Members</p>
<p>Award recipients</p>
<p>Ladies and gentlemen</p>
<p><strong>Opening</strong>
</p>
<p>Thank you, everyone for that very warm welcome. I am very glad to be here,
and I want to thank FBMA for having me.</p>
<p>FBMA was founded in 1989. What started as a way for food and beverage
professionals to gather has really grown to where the trade sets its standards,
trains its people, and speaks on behalf of the industry.</p>
<p>This is not a small sector. Food services employ about 240,000 workers
across close to 12,000 establishments. It is also one of the few industries
that almost every Singaporean interacts with every day.</p>
<p><strong>The Pinnacle Award</strong>
</p>
<p>Tonight, we present your inaugural Pinnacle Award, which recognises excellence,
professionalism and the people in F&amp;B. It covers five categories across
an F&amp;B career: Emerging Leader, Beverage Leader, Restaurant Manager,
Banquet Manager, and F&amp;B Leader. Together, they cover the whole journey,
from someone starting out to someone who sets the direction for others.</p>
<p>So I want to congratulate all the award recipients. I want to congratulate
FBMA as well for coming up with these awards and giving your members inspiration
and goals to work towards. I hope that all of you will continue to champion
this trade and inspire others to join the sector.</p>
<p><strong>Work-life harmony</strong>
</p>
<p>Dennis Ng (President of FMBA) spoke about work-life harmony and people,
and that is a very good segue for me because we do understand that attracting
and retaining people in this industry is not always easy, especially in
the last few years. You have to contend with rising costs, high rentals
and a tight labour market. But as we probe further, many also share that
the hours are long. Your peak hours are when others are enjoying themselves
over dinner or are at home. Your busiest days are weekends and public holidays,
when everyone else is with their families.</p>
<p>There is tension between two good things. On the one hand, your industry
runs on service excellence, and this takes years of hard work and commitment.
It is part of what makes Singapore’s food scene so dynamic and vibrant.
But the pursuit of this can sometimes also come at the expense of rest,
well-being and time with family. Both are important, and the question is
how they can co-exist.</p>
<p>In some sectors you can work from home, but it is difficult to do this
when you are in the frontline industry such as yours. But we think that
every sector can <strong>review its work processes</strong> to see how work
goals can be aligned with broader life goals.</p>
<p>The truth of the matter is, you want the people who are working for you
to be happy. Because if they are not happy, they leave. At the same time,
you have commercial goals, revenue targets and commercial objectives that
you need to achieve. The question is how to make this a win-win. We know
that, with the recent announcements, industries and companies have been
asking themselves how they are going to manage this. And that is also part
of the work that the Marriage &amp; Parenthood Reset Workgroup is looking
at. There are a few strategies. One of the key things is flexibility: flexibility
in how people work and in the workplace. It comes down to two things: when
people work, and how much—flexi-time and flexi-load. In the F&amp;B industry,
that could mean setting rosters early enough for people to plan their personal
commitments around them, letting shifts be swapped, or offering part-time
hours to those who want to keep working but cannot do full weeks.</p>
<p>To do this sustainably, employers can take the first step by speaking
to employees to understand their needs. Employer-employee conversations
are really important because, even among restaurants, the culture, operating
hours and work processes differ from restaurant to restaurant and organisation
to organisation. Having that conversation with employees and working something
out that meets your needs as well as theirs makes a big difference. We
also want to see how jobs can be redesigned. Through the SkillsFuture Workforce
Development Grant (Job Redesign+), employers can tap on Government support
to redesign jobs and transform their workforce. This is not a static area
because my Workgroup is looking at it, and we will see how we can improve.
One of the things we want to do is to work with your industry to understand
it better and see how we can collaborate with you.</p>
<p>We introduced the <strong>Tripartite Guidelines on Flexible Work Arrangement Requests</strong> to
set out the process for workers to formally request flexible work arrangements
and for employers to consider these requests on reasonable business grounds.
The guidelines are a step in the right direction. But supervisors and colleagues
are also very important in creating a workplace culture where employees
feel supported in utilising their parental leave and flexible work arrangements.
I also want to say that flexible work arrangements are not just for parents
with young children. There are singles who have to look after aged parents;
there are married couples who may not have children but have relatives
to look after; and there are those who have to look after young children
as well as aged parents. So basically, the workplace of today is very different
from the workplace of 10 or 20 years ago. Workplace norms and cultures
have to be rethought and redesigned.</p>
<p>Another area to think about is <strong>covering arrangements</strong>.
In office work, something may be able to wait.&nbsp; But in a restaurant,
a station cannot be left empty, and you need systematic covering arrangements.
And on costs, the Government has heard from all of you. We understand,
and that is why we have decided to reimburse employers for the financial
cost of all child-related leave, up to the reimbursement cap.&nbsp; This
significantly reduces the cost borne by employers, so we hope that you
will think about how to use the money you are able to save through this—either
to pay co-workers or cover covering arrangements—and also see how you can
backfill more easily.</p>
<p><strong>F&amp;B and family life</strong>
</p>
<p>Let me just end by saying that when we look at F&amp;B as a workplace,
there’s also F&amp;B as an industry.</p>
<p>Singaporeans spend more time eating out than cooking at home. According
to the Household Expenditure Survey, the average resident household spent
around $1,400 a month on food in 2023. Of that, around $950 went to meals
bought from restaurants, hawker centres, food courts, and coffee shops.
Close to 7 in every 10 food dollars is spent eating out.</p>
<p>F&amp;B outlets are important spaces for people to gather, build a sense
of community and relax outside the home. Everybody goes there: singles,
who build social connections; those who are married, who invest time in
their relationship; and families as well.</p>
<p>Singaporeans love their food, so F&amp;B places are really where they
hang out. You are very important social connectors. You provide places
where people can meet, build relationships and where families can thrive.
All of this would not be possible without your teams. So I just want to
encourage all of you—whether you run restaurants or other places where
people come together—to think about how to make them more family-friendly.
In my focus group discussions, many families tell me that there are not
enough places that are family-friendly and cater to young children, or
sometimes also to persons with disabilities and the elderly. If you want
the entire family to come out together, then the restaurants or food courts
must be family-friendly.</p>
<p><strong>The Marriage and Parenthood Reset</strong>
</p>
<p>And this connects to a larger national issue.</p>
<p>Singapore’s Total Fertility Rate fell to 0.87 last year. That was our
lowest to date. It means we will have far fewer people to support an ageing
population, and this will strain our workforce and economy if it does not
improve.</p>
<p>The Marriage and Parenthood Reset Workgroup, which I chair, was set up
to address this. PM Wong announced several recommendations last Sunday
at the National Day Rally. These include more protected time for parents,
as well as stronger financial support through a child’s growing years.</p>
<p>Policy alone will not be enough to move the needle. It really does have
to be a whole-of-society effort, where every organisation contributes within
its means. By “contribute”, I do not mean financially. It could simply
be the way you provide, for example, lactation rooms or fertility-friendly
policies. For fertility treatments, you are not considered sick, so you
cannot take sick leave. But actually, the process is very emotional and
stressful, and people do need time off. These are things we can discuss
later. But the point is that how we look at our workplaces, and how we
do things, makes a big difference over and above just policies. Those of
you who are employers have a say in whether your staff can build a family
life while working in this trade. And you are an industry where much of
that family life takes place.</p>
<p>So tonight, I just want to encourage the industry to focus on three things.
First, make rosters more predictable, where you can. Second, support your
staff when they need time for family, and put covering arrangements in
place. Third, make it easier for families with young children to dine with
you. These are small steps, but they can add up to big things.</p>
<p><strong>Closing</strong>
</p>
<p>So once again, thank you very much. I congratulate all the award recipients
and thank you for giving me this opportunity to be here.</p>
